# Sources — what the check-in reads, and how it judges what it reads

The forecast moves only on documents. This file is the standing registry: for every domain and every metric, the sources a check-in opens first, what to pull from each, how often they publish, and their tier (primary · institutional · peer-reviewed · quality-press · trade · vendor · weak). The same registry lives machine-readably in `data/sources.json`, which `npm run checkin:brief` reads for the standing queries. Audited 2026-10-23; every URL resolved that day. Refresh quarterly: moved or dead pages get replaced here and in the JSON, and a source that fails twice in a row is demoted.

Tiers, in the order a check-in trusts them: primary data (the measuring institution, regulator, legislature, or an independent evaluator) → institutional analysis → peer-reviewed → quality press with named reporting → trade press → vendor statements → everything else. A probability moves on the first three; the rest are leads.

## Where the forecast's own citations stand (audit of 2026-10-23)

87 distinct URLs are cited across `history`, `changelog` and `metrics`; all opened on 2026-10-23, none blocked, moved or dead (`data/citations.json` holds title, publisher, date, tier and status for each). Tier mix: institutional 20, primary 16, vendor 15, trade 14, weak 11, quality-press 9, peer-reviewed 2. Moves that still lean on weak or vendor sources and should be re-anchored at the next check-in: `the-silence` (a repost of the Grimaldi paper; cite the journal or arXiv version), `humanoids-in-homes` (vendor transcript and trade press only), `ai-drugs-phase-iii` / `ai-chemistry-majority` (sponsor press releases), `ai-runs-rd-programs` (Anthropic's own index), `superintelligence-regime` (a CEO essay plus press). Well anchored: the hiring, population, hinge and compute-talks moves.

## Avoid, or treat as leads only

| Pattern | Why | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Vendor newsrooms and CEO interviews for capability, deployment or schedule claims (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google launch posts, SpaceX, Figure, CFS, Helion, Insilico, Neuralink) | The claimant benefits; "deployed", "net energy", "AI-designed" are routinely redefined | Treat as a lead. Cite only when paired with an independent evaluation (METR/AISI/Epoch), a regulator record (FDA, FAA, ISO), or a customer on the record |
| SEO explainers and aggregator sites (e.g. "intuitionlabs", "gptprompts", exam-prep sites, "tech-insider") that top search results for FDA counts, state AI laws, IEA numbers | Second-hand, frequently wrong on dates and counts, undated updates | Go to the primary the page is summarising; never cite the summary |

## Source rules for a check-in

1. **One primary or it did not happen.** A probability moves only on a document from the measuring institution, regulator, court, legislature, or an independent evaluator (tier primary/institutional/peer-reviewed). Press and vendor posts are leads that must resolve to such a document; if they cannot, log the lead in the changelog body, not in `sources`.
2. **A single vendor claim is a signal, never a resolution.** It can move a `reading` by at most 0.1 or a `probability` by at most 2 points, and only when the claim is specific (units, dates, customers) and the history entry says "vendor claim, unverified". Two independent vendors making the same claim are still one claim.
3. **Independent evaluation beats self-report; the stricter metric beats the looser.** For capability, prefer METR/AISI/Epoch over lab benchmarks; for "deployed", prefer regulator or grid-operator records over announcements; for jobs, prefer BLS/NY Fed over company statements.
4. **Measure against the forecast's own definition.** Before citing, restate the line's test (e.g. "net electricity to a grid" = metered export confirmed by the ISO or utility). If the source answers a different question, it does not count, however impressive.
5. **Paywalled primaries are fine, paywalled press is not.** A paywalled journal article or report is citable with DOI/URL, title, date and the exact sentence relied on in `history.reason`. A paywalled news story is citable only if no underlying document exists, and the history entry marks it `paywalled` so a reader knows what they cannot verify.
6. **Prediction-market moves count only with a named cause.** Record a market when it diverges from the line by more than 15 points, then find the event behind it; the event's source is what goes in `sources`. Never cite a market price as the reason for a change.
7. **Cite datasets by edition and access date.** Format: `Publisher, Dataset (revision/edition), accessed YYYY-MM-DD, URL`. UN WPP 2024 vs 2026, BLS preliminary vs revised, Epoch CSV date, Copernicus bulletin month all change under you; `observed` points in metrics.json should carry the edition in their note.
8. **One month is weather; twelve months is a reading.** For warming, prices, hiring and benchmarks, cite the running annual or multi-benchmark figure. A record month can be mentioned but cannot move a probability by itself.
9. **Conflicting primaries: cite both, move toward the more conservative.** When Copernicus and NOAA, or GAO and NASA, disagree, record both numbers in the history entry and let the forecast lean on the slower-moving one.
10. **Three-source rule for anything that resolves a line.** Marking `happened`, `partial`, `early`, `late` or `missed` needs at least two independent primaries (or one primary and one peer-reviewed/institutional confirmation) plus a date, all in `history.sources`.
11. **Check that you actually opened it.** If a page is bot-blocked from the cloud run (BLS, CISA, congress.gov, OECD, IEA, CDC, NCSL, GAO), use the alternative host or search→fetch route listed in the registry; if it still does not open, do not cite it and leave a note in the changelog body for the desktop run to verify.
12. **Say what would have changed your mind.** Every history entry naming a source should also say what the source would have had to show to move the line the other way. It keeps the sourcing honest and makes the grade legible later.
13. **Refresh the registry quarterly.** Dead links, moved pages (BIS press page, FDA guidance URLs, NASA mission pages all moved this year), and new trackers go in `sources.json`; a source that fails twice in a row is demoted or replaced, with a changelog note.
